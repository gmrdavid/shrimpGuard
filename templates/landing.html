Species-Specific Engineering

Why generic pond monitoring fails shrimp aquaculture.

Pacific White Shrimp (Penaeus vannamei) are delicate, high-density organisms. Unlike finfish, their molting physiology, ionic osmoregulation, and feed tray consumption vary drastically under minute shifts in water chemistry.

ShrimpGuard was engineered from the ground up to model the nonlinear interaction between water temperature, diurnal pH swings, and salinity—safeguarding against toxic unionized ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) spikes before physical mortality occurs.

Water Temperature Strict Window
26.0 – 32.0 °C

Temperatures below 24°C stall metabolism; above 33°C hyper-accelerates respiration and ammonia excretion.

pH Stability Range Equilibrium
7.50 – 8.50 pH

Maintains carapace integrity and prevents extreme swings that convert ammonium into lethal unionized ammonia.

Salinity (Osmoregulation) Brackish
15.0 – 25.0 ppt

Optimal isosmotic zone minimizing metabolic expenditure on ion pumping, maximizing feed conversion.

Unionized Ammonia (NH³) Critical Limit
< 0.05 mg/L

Modeled via Van Wyk & Scarpa dissociation. Values above 0.1 mg/L cause gill necrosis and mass mortality.
